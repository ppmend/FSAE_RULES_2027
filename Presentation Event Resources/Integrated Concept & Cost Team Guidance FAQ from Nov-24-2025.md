Definitions

Sponsorship Proposal Request – the Presentation Event Concept, made of a Written Sponsorship Proposal and a Presentation. May be referred to as Proposal in this document.

Request for Quote – the submission for the Cost and Manufacturing Scenario. May be referred to as RFQ in this document. The intention of this RFQ is to provide it to a potential sponsor who should be able to quote, build, and deliver the system without further instruction.

Part I – Summarized and consolidated Q&A from live meeting

1\. ROI, KPIs, Metrics, and Realism 1.1 What is ROI in this event context?

ROI refers to return on investment, usually expressed financially. However, teams may also include quantifiable non-financial returns such as time savings, manufacturability improvements, performance gains, or marketing value.

1.2 Can teams invent ROI or KPI metrics?

Yes—teams may develop their own metrics or scenarios. These must be:

- Realistic
- Logically justified
- Consistent with industry reasoning
- Defensible if questioned

Extreme or unsubstantiated assumptions will result in lower scores.

2\. Event Documents, Scoring, and Questions 2.1 Where can we find the concept, rubrics, and guidelines?

All official event materials are available at fsaeonline.com under Series Resources.

2.2 How do we know what we are evaluated on?

The scoring rubric is authoritative for all evaluation criteria.

2.3 Where can we ask follow-up questions after meetings?

Submit them through the official rules inquiry system on fsaeonline.com. A specific answer may not be provided privately to your team. Common questions may be published publicly.

3\. Audience & Sponsor Selection 3.1 Who is the audience for the Proposal and RFQ?

The audience is defined by the concept. Judges act as representatives of the sponsor(s) you identify.

3.2 Can sponsors be real, fictional, or multiple organizations?

Yes. Sponsors may be:

- Real companies
- Fictional companies
- A mix of both
- Multiple sponsors

Teams must clearly define the audience and maintain consistency throughout their documents.

3.3 Does sponsor recognition matter?

No. Realism matters more than brand recognition. Judges will not penalize teams for choosing local or lesser-known companies.

3.4 Can we use our existing sponsors?

Yes.

3.5 Must the sponsor be directly suspension-related?

No. Monetary, material, or service sponsorships are permitted from any source if it supports the subsystem.

4\. Written Proposal Requirements 4.1 Should the Proposal look like a report or a marketing brochure?

Format is flexible. You will be evaluated on:

- Clarity
- Technical soundness
- Visual communication
- Completeness per the rubric

Choose the format that best supports your pitch.

4.2 How technical should the Proposal document be?

Include enough detail to justify decisions but avoid unnecessary depth. Depth should align with the rubric—not a full engineering design review.

4.3 Are references or citations required in the Proposal?

No formal bibliography. External information may be integrated into the narrative as appropriate.

4.4 How should the 8-page limit be used?

Focus on covering all required elements (value proposition, sponsor alignment, subsystem description, ROI, KPIs, etc.) concisely and professionally. Remember: Judges will only review the first 8 pages; additional pages will not be reviewed or scored.

5\. Presentation Round Integration 5.1 Should the Presentation expand on the written report?

Yes—reinforce and enhance your strongest points while maintaining alignment with the written submission.

5.2 Should written and Presentation judges be the same?

Not necessarily, but Presentation judges have access to your written proposal.

5.3 Can we modify the written proposal after advancing?

You cannot change the submitted written document, but your presentation can refine and highlight content without contradicting it.

6\. Suspension Subsystem Scope 6.1 What is included in the “suspension subsystem”?

Refer to the FSAE Cost Supplement for the official definition. Typically includes:

- Control arms
- Uprights
- Dampers & springs
- Rockers / pushrods
- Anti-roll bar components
- Bearings, bushings
- Fasteners
- Raw materials
- Sensors only if they remain on the vehicle as part of the operational subsystem

6.2 Does the subsystem include steering or chassis?

No, only elements included in the suspension definition per the FSAE Cost Supplement.

7\. Consistency with Vehicle and Cost Event 7.1 Must RFQ’d parts be installed on the vehicle?

Yes. Components in the proposal must exist on the competition vehicle or be extremely close variants. Minor updates (e.g., material upgrades) must be justified.

7.2 Must Presentation, Cost Report, and RFQ match?

They should be highly consistent. Minor discrepancies are acceptable if well-supported with documentation.

8\. Cost RFQ (Request for Quote) Requirements 8.1 Does the RFQ cover the whole subsystem or one component?

The RFQ must cover the entire suspension subsystem, not a single component.

8.2 Must fasteners and raw materials be included?

Yes—everything needed to quote, build, and deliver the complete subsystem as RFQ’d.

8.3 Can the RFQ be addressed to a fictional company?

Yes. It may be addressed to:

- A real vendor
- A fictional vendor

8.4 Do we need multiple RFQs if we have multiple sponsors?

You may use:

- One RFQ for a single supplier, or
- Separate RFQs for each sponsor

Either method is acceptable. Remember the RFQ submission is for Cost event only. Additionally, regardless of creating a one or multiple RFQ’s, the team must merge all RFQ’s into a single merged PDF for submission online and is submission file size must be equal to or less than 25MB.

8.5 Should assembly instructions include installing the subsystem on the car?

No. Scope is limited to the suspension subsystem assembly, not vehicle installation.

8.6 Can the RFQ include jigs, fixtures, or templates?

Yes—if required to manufacture or assemble the subsystem components.

8.7 Are appendices allowed?

Appendices in RFQs are allowed only as part of the Cost Addendum, following Cost Event rules.

9\. Manufacturing, Labor, and Assembly Expectations 9.1 Can sponsors assemble the subsystem?

Yes. Teams may request:

- Full subsystem assembly
- Component manufacturing only
- Materials only
- Monetary support

RFQ must clearly define the scope.

9.2 What about custom or in-house machined parts?

Teams must provide all information needed for a sponsor to quote and build the subsystem—even if your team normally manufactures those parts in-house.

9.3 Should labor setup costs be included in ROI?

Include setup labor if relevant. The Cost supplement specifies how setup is costed, but for the RFQ the team isn’t using the Cost Supplement outside defining what the Suspension System is.

10\. Sponsorship Types Allowed 10.1 Can teams pursue monetary sponsorship instead of parts in the Proposal?

Yes. Monetary sponsorship is allowed if the funds support the suspension subsystem.

10.2 Can teams seek sponsorship for testing or facilities in the Proposal?

Testing that supports the subsystem is allowed, but only components and labor to create the subsystem belong in the RFQ.

11\. Multi-Sponsor Scenarios 11.1 Can we request sponsorship from multiple companies in the Proposal?

Yes. Just clearly communicate how each sponsor fits into the overall proposal.

11.2 Does the RFQ also need multiple sponsors if the presentation does?

Not necessarily. The Proposal and the RFQ do not need to map 1:1.

12\. Competition Logistics & Administration 12.1 Does being waitlisted affect advancement?

No. Waitlisted teams may still advance to the virtual presentation round based on written proposal scores.

12.2 Are IC and EV entries judged together?

No. Each category is evaluated independently.

12.3 Can a non-running car make the presentation finals?

Yes. Presentation Finals do not require the vehicle’s presence. However, the Cost Event requires the vehicle on-site to score points.

12.4 What defines “sponsorship”?

Teams define the sponsorship model—financial, material, manufacturing, or service-based—provided it fits event rules.

12.5 Are we pitching as a business or as an FSAE team?

You are pitching as an FSAE team seeking sponsorship to support completion of your suspension subsystem.

13\. Deadlines & Results 13.1 When will we hear back about advancing to the virtual round?

Judges will have 4–6 weeks to review, score, and provide feedback on the written submission. SAE will release a list of teams advancing to the virtual presentation round before the static schedule with assigned times is published. IC teams can expect notification of advancement in February 2026, while EV teams will be notified in March 2026. Teams should prepare to begin virtual presentation development shortly after written evaluations conclude.

- 1\. What do you mean by ROI? Are they strictly financial or more loosely
    defined as just benefits?
    ROI is typically a financial metric (return on investment), best when quantitative (e.g., dollars, time savings), but can include other measurable benefits. The more quantifiable, the better.
- 2\. Where do we find the Sponsorship Proposal Request and any guidelines
    for our written response? How do we know exactly what we are scored on?
    The event documents, including the concept and scoring rubric, are posted on fsaeonline.com under Series Resources. Teams are encouraged to review the scoring rubric as a guide for their sponsor proposal request.
- 3\. Who is the audience for the sponsorship proposal—judges or sponsors?
    Should we have separate asks for each sponsor?
    The audience is defined in the concept; judges act as representatives of the sponsor company/companies. You may pitch to multiple sponsors if you believe that is the direction best for your team however you need to be sure define your audience and approach in your proposal.
- 4\. Could you clarify the expectations for the virtual presentation? Are we
    expected to build and drastically expand on our written response or simply reformulate and add a few specifics? This kind of links to our second question, are the judges scoring our written response the same ones who will judge our presentation? If no, how acquainted will the judges be with our written response?
    Expand on your best points and use feedback from the written round to improve your virtual presentation but do not deviate from the written proposal as the sponsor proposal and virtual presentation pitch should align. Judges for the written and virtual rounds may differ, but virtual judges have access to review written proposals beforehand for their assigned teams.
- 5\. The rulebook states that the suspension component we are talking about

- 6\. Should fasteners be included in the suspension package RFQ? Are they

    The RFQ should capture all items required for the system, including fasteners. If a specific supplier is needed, provide that information. The sponsor should be able to quote, build, and deliver the system without further info.

    Refer to the cost supplement for the definition. The RFQ should cover the entire system, not just a single component.

8\. How are metrics like ROI/KPIs judged? Accuracy, against the industry

average, can we just make them up basically?

You can create scenarios and metrics, but they should be believable and logically justified. Judges look for realistic, defensible numbers.

    You may choose either real or fictional sponsors. Define your audience and approach accordingly.
- 12\. Where to ask questions after the meeting? Submit questions via the rule’s inquiry process; frequently asked questions may be posted for all teams.

    Yes, the intent is to cover the entire subsystem as defined in the cost supplement.
- 15\. How to handle custom/machined parts in sponsorship requests? You may seek sponsorship from a machining company or request materials for in-house manufacturing. Clearly specify what is needed.

- 17\. Does initial setup for manufacturing need to be detailed in ROI? Setup labor can be included if relevant; in cost, it is typically rolled into tooling costs. Clarify your assumptions.

71\. Why are we being asked to generate an RFQ for the Cost Scenario?

Writing an RFQ to submit to a supplier is an important step that occurs several times in the product development lifecycle. While most of the design, manufacturing, and assembly tasks are performed by students in Formula SAE, this is often not the case in industry. Sourcing supplier(s) to build a prototype (such as the Formula SAE vehicle’s suspension system) requires thorough documentation via BOMs, drawings, and other information included in a data package. Developing the team’s skills to communicate a data package to a third party is the primary goal of the Cost Scenario for 2026.

Teams will note that a lot of the information required in the RFQ is very similar to information that is in the Cost Report; this is intentional. Many of the Drawings and BOM could be reused from the Report to the Scenario, though the detail, format, and presentation of that information may differ. To assist the teams with evaluating whether the submission is sufficient, a list of questions was provided at the end of the Integrated Presentation Concept and Cost Scenario document.
